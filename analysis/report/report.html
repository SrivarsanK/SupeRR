1. What counts as pressure

Pressure is about the state of the match before the ball is bowled, not about what happens on it. I rebuilt the score, wickets, legal balls and target for every delivery and combined three signals into a pressure index from 0 to 1.

  • Required rate (chases only). Six an over counts as routine and fifteen as extreme, scaled in between. A chase that needs more than 18 an over, or is already won, scores zero: the result is no longer in doubt.
  • Collapse. Wickets lost compared with the average for that over and innings. Three wickets above par is the maximum.
  • Time left. How far through the innings the ball is. The same situation is harder to fix with four balls left than with forty.

Chasing: 0.5 required rate + 0.2 collapse + 0.3 time. Batting first: 0.5 collapse + 0.5 time, because there is no target yet. Knockout matches get a 10% uplift. The top quarter of balls by index (index of 0.46 or more) are high pressure; that is 50,243 balls.

2. How players are rated

Value of each ball

A strike rate of 160 in the 19th over of a chase is not the same as 160 in the powerplay. So each ball is first compared with what an average player did in the same context: same format (IPL or T20I), innings, over and pressure band. The batter's value is runs above that average, minus 6 runs for a dismissal above the average rate. The bowler's value is the mirror image: runs saved, plus 6 runs a wicket. Byes and leg-byes are not charged to the bowler; wides and no-balls are.

Pressure Elo

Every high-pressure ball is a duel. The batter scores 0.5 for an average ball, more for a good one, close to 0 if out; the bowler gets the rest. Both start at 1500, and ratings move after each ball in date order. The expected score depends on the rating gap, so runs off a top bowler count for more than runs off a weak one, and the same for wickets. The step size is small (K = 2 a ball) because one ball tells you little; of the step sizes I tried (2, 4 and 8), 2 gave the most repeatable ratings.

Baseline

The same Elo run on normal balls gives each player's usual level. The tables show both, so you can see who rises and who simply keeps their level. The ranking itself uses the pressure Elo, because the brief asks who delivers most when it matters, not who improves the most.

Fair comparisons

  • Sample size. At least 120 high-pressure balls across 12 or more matches. Elo itself starts everyone at 1500 and only moves them with evidence, so short records stay close to average.
  • Active players. At least one match since January 2025.
  • Players RR can sign. Pakistan internationals are left out of the rankings because IPL teams cannot sign them. They still count as opponents in everyone else's ratings. Without this rule Sufiyan Muqeem and Abrar Ahmed would be 2nd and 5th among bowlers. Andre Russell is left out for the same reason: he retired from the IPL in November 2025. He would otherwise be 6th among batters.
  • IPL and T20I. Both are used. Averages are set separately for each, and the Elo rewards beating strong opponents, so runs against weaker T20I attacks count for less.
  • Match context. Rain-revised targets and overs are used for the chase. Super overs are left out.

That leaves 97 batters and 112 bowlers who qualify.

5. Player profiles

Why each player is on the list. Each profile compares his high-pressure balls with his normal balls, and the "Rank of" column places his high-pressure figure among every qualified player (97 batters, 112 bowlers). Rank 1 is best; for a batter's dot balls, and a bowler's economy, balls per wicket and boundary balls, the lowest number ranks first. "Place in the order" gives his pressure-Elo gap to the players either side, which is what sets his rank; for the 10th player it is the margin over the first player left out. The profile also splits the pressure balls by situation, shows every year with at least 20 pressure balls, and names his best pressure match. "Runs above average" (batters) and "runs saved" (bowlers) are the ball values from section 2, per 100 balls. Roles follow simple rules on the data: a batter is a finisher if he usually bats at 6 or lower or takes 45% or more of his pressure balls in overs 16-20; a bowler is a death bowler if half or more of his pressure balls are in overs 16-20.

6. What this means for RR

RR's own 2026 IPL season, rated the same way, ranked 4 of 10 for batting under pressure and 8 of 10 for bowling under pressure. The bowling gap was in the middle overs: 9.2 an over under pressure against a league average of 8.5. At the death RR conceded 10.5 against 10.5, a little better than average. (578 high-pressure balls, so treat this as a direction, not a verdict.)

So the most useful signings are bowlers who can hold the middle overs while a chase is still alive, and that is where the spinners on this list do most of their work.

Retention lists for IPL 2027 were not public when I wrote this, so whether a player reaches the auction is not known yet.

7. Reliability and limitations

Do the lists hold if I change the judgement calls?

A wicket worth 8 runs9/108/10

Is it a real skill or luck?

I split the matches into two halves (odd and even) and rated players on each half separately. If pressure performance is a skill, the two ratings should agree. For batters the pressure Elo correlates at 0.40 across halves, against 0.24 for a simple runs-above-average score. For bowlers it is 0.15 against -0.03.

  • The batting list is reasonably stable. The bowling list is not: Narine is clear, and ranks 2 to 10 sit within six Elo points.
  • Ranks 4 to 10 in both lists are within a few Elo points of each other.
  • Elo moves further with more balls, so it favours players with long records over short, strong ones.
  • Taskin Ahmed has no IPL matches in the data, so his rating comes from T20Is only. The opponent adjustment helps, but there is less evidence than for IPL regulars.
  • In a first innings the time signal alone puts the last two overs above the threshold, whatever the score. Those overs are hard to bat in, but the label is automatic there.
  • The data has no fielding positions, field settings or Impact Player tactics, and 2022 to 2026 is a short window.

8. How to reproduce

python analysis/pressure.py --check rebuilds the match state, the ratings and the checks, and writes every eligible player to batters.csv and bowlers.csv. python analysis/report.py builds this report. Both use only the data pack, pandas, numpy and matplotlib.

The code, this report and the dashboard are at github.com/SrivarsanK/SupeRR (the data pack itself is not included, as the contest rules require). Dashboard: rr-pressure-ranking.vercel.app.
